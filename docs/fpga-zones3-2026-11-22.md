# Our first SNN running on the FPGA: target-zone network, 22 Nov 2026

The ball detector U-Net hung the KV260's accelerator (`docs/fpga-run-2026-11-22.md`): even an
empty frame never came back, while Durance's example worked on the same board. The
suspect left was the architecture (320x320 input, skips, upscaling, 9x80x80 output). So on
the morning of 22 Nov we trained a network with the shape of Durance's example (2x80x80 in,
a chain of 5 convolutions, a small output) and put it on the board.

Result:

- **It runs.** Every frame comes back, in 1-7 ms. This is the first network of ours that works
  on the FPGA.
- **It computes what we trained.** With synthetic frames, the board's output spikes match the
  laptop's fixed-point emulation neuron by neuron for the first 6 frames, then stay close.
- **`weights.txt` must be two's complement.** riverops 0.2.0 exports signed weights. The
  board reads them wrongly.
- **The neuron state carries over** from frame to frame and from one client connection to the
  next.
- **Fed by the real camera** (section 9): 100 frames per second, about 1.2 ms each, decision
  `none` with no ball in view; with balls thrown past it, brief `left`/`center`/`right`
  decisions, accuracy not measured (throws not noted). Not connected to the robot.

The model, its data, its training and its validation are in `models/zones3/PROVENANCE.md`.
The firmware is in `models/firmware/kv260_zones3_job44/PROVENANCE.md`.

## 1. The network

`reflex/river/` (Philipp's port of Durance's Motion Buddy notebook, from branch `merge-all`).
It answers, per 10 ms frame: is there a ball, and in which third of the image.

| | Ball detector (hangs) | Durance's example (works) | **zones3 (works)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input | 1x320x320 | 2x80x80 | **2x80x80** |
| Layers | 14 convolutions, 3 skips, 3 upscaling | 5 convolutions in a chain | **5 convolutions in a chain** |
| Output | 9x80x80 (position heatmap) | 5x1x1 (gesture class) | **4x1x1** (`none / left / center / right`) |
| Camera config | `frequency` 500, `downsampling` 1 | `frequency` 100, `downsampling` 4 | **`frequency` 100, `downsampling` 4** |
| Firmware job | #35 | Durance's | **#44** |

zones3 layers: 2->16 k5 s2 (40x40), 16->32 k3 s2 (20x20), 32->32 k3 s2 (10x10), 32->64 k3 s2
(5x5), 64->4 k5 (1x1). LIF with a fixed leak of 0.93, threshold 1.0, hard reset. 39,752
parameters.

## 2. Training

Data: the 21 Nov recordings with the real GenX320 on a stand (one room, one evening). The
ball comes in from the left, from the right or from the center (`sat_cross1`, `sat_cross2`),
plus takes without a ball (`no-ball`, `none`). The auto-labeller gives every 10 ms frame its
zone. 49 ball and 19 none takes for training, 11 and 4 for validation. Exact takes in
`models/zones3/PROVENANCE.md`.

```bash
python3 tools/river_layout.py data/recordings_2026-11-21 data/recordings_2026-11-21_cross --out data/river
pixi run --manifest-path tools/river/pixi.toml python -u -m reflex.river.train \
  --data data/river --val-sessions val --test-sessions \
  --out out/river/zones3_v1 --epochs 15 --samples-per-epoch 4000 --workers 8 \
  > out/river/zones3_v1.log 2>&1
```

15 epochs on CPU, about 1 minute each. Best epoch 9. Validation, fixed point as on the board:

| | none | left | center | right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recall | 62 % | 89 % | 83 % | 90 % |

Balanced accuracy 81.2 %, false trigger (none -> a zone) 38 %, miss 4 %, wrong zone 9 %.
Validation is optimistic: same room, and the 2 still-scene takes are in both splits. False
triggers are the weak point.

## 8. Conclusions

1. **The hang was our architecture.** The same board and server, with a 2x80x80 -> chain ->
   4x1x1 network, answer every frame. What exactly in the U-Net broke the hardware (320x320
   input, skips, upscaling or the 9x80x80 output) is still not separated.
2. **`weights.txt`: weights and biases in two's complement, 0-255.** That is the encoding of
   Durance's example `weights.txt`. Threshold and leak (16-bit Q4.12, positive) stay as they
   are. riverops 0.2.0's `export_params` gives signed values (-128..127). Written that way,
   the board computes something else. `reflex/river/train.py` now writes two's complement
   (`board_weights_text`, test in `tests/test_river.py`). It produces exactly the file
   tested in B (sha256 `65bacbcc…`).
   Biases are 8-bit Q1.7 here. Durance's example has 16-bit biases (values such as 4015 and
   64983). Ours work as 8-bit, as B shows.
3. **The neuron state carries over.** The board's output changes from frame to frame for
   identical frames, as it does in the emulation with the state kept. The training assumes
   that (the gesture example needs it too). The state also survives a client disconnect: the
   same frames gave different answers in 5.2 and A without a restart. For a repeatable test,
   `systemctl restart spleat-server` first. With the camera that does not matter, it streams
   without a pause.
4. **Empty frames change the state too.** In the emulation, 5 empty frames before the dense
   ones delay and thin the first spikes (only 2 spikes in 10 frames instead of 12). The
   reason is probably the biases from folding BatchNorm. Not measured on the board separately.
5. **Latency**: 0.3-1.7 ms per empty frame, 3.5-5.4 ms per dense frame (`latency`, µs).
   Durance's example: 0.5-30 ms. At 100 frames per second (10 ms per frame) there is time
   to spare.

## 10. What despot sends: the sum, and filtered

In a closed-loop run in simulation (section 11) the dog leaned left 5 times and never right;
the window counts were nearly tied (`none 12 left 13 center 9 right 3`). Two mismatches between
despot's frames and the training data were found, and both were fixed in the board's
configuration, without retraining.

**Capture** (server stopped, `tools/spleat_sniff.py` on 3333, despot as configured, ball moved
left and right for 15 s):

```
message 0: 51216 bytes   as float32: 12800 values, shapes ['2x80x80'], min 0.0, max 17.0, nonzero 8000, 17 distinct: [0.0, 1.0, 2.0, ...]
message 1: 51216 bytes   as float32: 12800 values, shapes ['2x80x80'], min 0.0, max 2.0, nonzero 67, 3 distinct: [0.0, 1.0, 2.0]
```

1080 frames in 15 s (72 per second), integer values 0-17, median 3 events per frame.

1. **The sum, not the mean.** despot sends the number of events per 4x4 block. The network was
   trained on that number / 16 (`norm mean`), so it saw 16 times too much, and values above 8
   saturate Q4.12. spleat-server multiplies its input by `scaling` to make it fixed point, so
   `scaling: 256` (4096 / 16) gives the trained values. Checked with `spleat_poke.py` against
   the emulation of the same frame at 1/16:

   | | frames 0-9 |

## 11. The whole pipeline on the Kria

Everything runs on the board; only the robot changes (the real Go2, or the MuJoCo dog on the
laptop standing in for it):

```
GenX320 -> despot -3333-> camera_tap -3334-> spleat-server (FPGA) -> fpga_perception -> behavior -> /api/sport/request
                              +-> /reflex/debug/events_image (ros-web)        robot:=real -> Go2 (USB-Ethernet)
                                                                              robot:=sim  -> laptop (eth0)
```

- `fpga_perception` (new): reads the server's output on 3334, `zones_readout`, publishes
  `/reflex/decision` (confidence = winner's spikes / 20). It takes the `perception` node's place.
- `camera_tap` (new): between despot and the server, forwards every frame (reordered CHW -> HWC,
  section 12) and
  publishes what the network sees (80x80, 10 Hz, image thirds marked) for the browser. A first
  version at 240x240 and 15 Hz lagged the whole pipeline (Python held while building the
  picture, frames queued behind it); at 80x80 it uses 5 % CPU and the server gets about 107
  frames per second.
- `behavior`: unchanged. With `robot:=real` it starts disabled (`enabled:=true` on purpose).
- `kria.launch.py` starts these and despot (3 s after camera_tap) and pins CycloneDDS to the
  Go2's interface (`/etc/cyclonedds/cyclonedds-go2.xml`) or to `eth0`.
- `/opt/reflex/run.sh` runs `despot_test_camera` first (unless despot already runs), moves
  spleat-server to 3334 if needed, sources ROS, Durance's overlay (for `unitree_api`) and ours.

Built for the board with Durance's Docker image (`durance/go2-yocto:humble`, from their
`go2-kria` folder and the x86_64 SDK installer, sha256 `ae40d393…`): `reflex_msgs` (aarch64,
Python 3.12) and `reflex_ros`. `unitree_api` comes from Durance's runtime in `/opt/go2-*`
(message definitions identical to ours). `scripts/kria/deploy.sh` builds, packs (660 KB) and
installs into `/opt/reflex`.

```bash
# laptop, once: ./manage.sh image in Durance's go2-kria folder (sdk/installer.sh = the x86_64 SDK)
scripts/kria/deploy.sh                                              # build + install on the board
pixi run -e ros ros-robot-sim behavior:=false dds_interface:=enp8s0 # laptop: the dog, robot only
CYCLONEDDS_URI='<CycloneDDS><Domain Id="any"><General><Interfaces><NetworkInterface name="enp8s0"/></Interfaces></General></Domain></CycloneDDS>' pixi run -e ros ros-web
ssh -t root@192.168.77.1 /opt/reflex/run.sh robot:=sim             # the board: everything else
```

Seen in simulation: decisions over the cable to the laptop, the simulated dog leaning on
`left` decisions (5 times in about a minute, before the input fixes). Not yet tried with the
real Go2.

Board quirks met on the way: no `timeout`, no `pkill`, BusyBox `ps`/`head`. Stopping the launch
sometimes leaves nodes behind (an orphaned `fpga_perception` on the server slowed the frames);
check with `ps | grep -E "despot_send|reflex_ros"` before starting again.

## 12. The FPGA reads its input as HWC; despot sends CHW

With the scale and filter fixed, live decisions were still weak and tied. To separate the
board from the camera and the scene, `tools/replay_to_fpga.py` sends labelled takes from the
21 Nov recordings to the server, framed like despot (sum per 4x4 block and polarity, 10 ms,
2x80x80), and applies the same readout as `fpga_perception`. The laptop's fixed-point emulation
gets the same frames. Share of frames per decision (validation takes of sat_cross1):

| Take | emulation | board, CHW (as despot) | board, HWC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 004 from-left | left 69 % | left 12 %, none 72 % | **left 65 %** |
| 009 from-left | left 83 % | left 23 %, center 27 % | **left 80 %** |
| 014 from-right | right 69 % | right 1 %, none 89 % | center 65 %, right 26 % |
| 019 from-right | right 76 % | right 10 %, center 52 % | **right 61 %** |
| 024 from-center | center 48 % | none 100 % | **center 96 %** |
| 034, 039 no-ball | none 91 %, 31 % | none 99-100 % | **none 100 %** |

So the FPGA (or the server) takes the 12800 values as row, column, channel (HWC: the two
polarities of a pixel next to each other), while despot sends channel, row, column (CHW, as
`camera_tap` shows: a coherent picture when read as CHW). The synthetic tests of section 5
used the same frame in both channels, which is the same in both layouts, so they could not
show it. Durance's example model gets despot's frames the same way; whether it has the same
problem is not known.

Ruled out on the way, with the same replay: `scaling` is a plain input multiplier (scaling 256
with sums gives byte-identical answers to scaling 4096 with sums / 16); and rounding in layer 0
(input, bias and threshold of layer 0 times 16, equivalent in exact arithmetic, changed
nothing; file kept as `models/zones3/weights_sum_input.txt`, not deployed).

Fix: `camera_tap` reorders every frame to HWC before forwarding (`to_hwc`, default on). 107
frames per second at 5 % CPU.

Live after the fix, in simulation (`run.sh robot:=sim`, then `min_confidence:=0.25`): `left`,
`center` and `right` all occur now (before: almost only `left`), no "perception is silent";
the simulated dog leaned right and crouched on `center`. But the winner rarely fires in more
than 9 of 20 frames and the four counts stay close, so with the default min_confidence 0.45
it reacted once in about a minute. On the recordings the board now does about what the
emulation does; with today's scene it does not. The likely reason is the training data: one
room, one evening, one camera position. Recording takes in the current setup and retraining
(same architecture, weights only, no new firmware) is the next step if there is time.
