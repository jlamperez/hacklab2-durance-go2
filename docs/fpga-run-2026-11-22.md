# First runs of the small ball detector on the FPGA, 22 Nov 2026

The small ball detector (`models/ball_small`, early QAT checkpoint, firmware job #35) was
deployed on Durance's KV260 on 21 Nov (`docs/deploy-fpga.md`). On 22 Nov it was fed real
event-camera frames for the first time. Result: **the server accepts our frames, one
inference came back from the FPGA, then the hardware hung.** This page keeps the logs.

The board's clock is wrong (it says 30 May 2025); the timestamps below are the board's.

DMA interrupts (`grep xilinx-dma /proc/interrupts`; line 57 is transmit, 56 is receive: with

| Ours, after boot -> after 1 frame | 2 -> 6 | 0 -> 2 |

Our 400 kB frame goes out in 4 transfers (theirs: 1). Two receive transfers complete, then
nothing until the timeout. A reading, not a check: the accelerator starts sending its output
and never finishes, so backward-server never sees the end-of-inference event.

## What it shows

- The input side works: despot -> adapter -> server -> FPGA, with frames the server accepts.
- **The board is fine; our bitstream (job #35) is not.** Durance's example answers 10 frames
  out of 10 on the same board. Ours hangs on the first frame after a clean boot, even an
  empty one. The single reply we got (`latency: 219331`, 219 ms, about 10 times Durance's
  slowest) was the first frame of the first run.
- Not the camera, not the adapter, not flooding, not the amount of output: an empty frame
  sent directly hangs too.
- Suspects, not checked: the 9x80x80 spatial output or the 320x320 input. Durance's example
  is 2x80x80 in, 5x1x1 out, and spatial outputs had never been tried on the FPGA. Why the
  output is the first suspect is below.

## Why the spatial output is the first suspect

The two kinds of output, by their shape (channels x height x width):

| | Shape | Values | Bytes per message | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classification, Durance's example | 5x1x1 | 5 | 20 | one score per class for the whole image |
| Spatial, our ball detector | 9x80x80 | 57600 | 230400 | a map: 9 values for each of 80x80 positions (each covers 4x4 of the 320x320 pixels); the readout turns it into `x`, `y`, `P(ball)` |

- The error is on the **read side**: `error reading through dma: rx hardware status:
  ProxyTimeout` comes when the server reads the result back from the FPGA.
- Our result is about 11500 times larger than the example's. An output this size has never
  been run on SPLEAT, and Durance's own ROS client drops messages above 64 kB.
- If the bitstream writes a different amount than the DMA transfer expects, or the buffer is
  too small, a first read could still come back and the next one stall: what we saw.

This is reasoning from the error, not a check. The 320x320 input (16 times the example's
80x80) is the other candidate. An empty frame hangs too (section 5), so it is not the
*amount* of output spikes; it may still be the output's *shape* (the accelerator never
finishing a 9x80x80 result).

## Would a lower camera frequency help?

`frequency` in `/root/metavision_camera.json` sets the frame window: 500 -> 2 ms, 200 -> 5 ms.

- **The hang, probably not.** The second attempt failed with one frame in flight, so the rate
  of frames is not the cause. The one frame that did come back was the densest one (87522
  active pixels), so density does not seem to break it either.
- **The rate barely changes**: despot delivered about 80 frames per second while asked for 500.
- **The model does change**: the deployed checkpoint was trained on 2 ms windows, hence 500.
  At 200 the frames hold about 2.5 times more events than in training. 200 is right for the
  checkpoint trained on 5 ms windows, once it is deployed.
